Systematic technology review

Protective technologies for bridges

Review on the protective technologies of bridge against vessel collision

Thin-Walled Structures 201, 112013 (2024) Active avoidancePassive protectionTechnology comparison DOI

Research question

How should available vessel-collision protection technologies be classified, compared, and selected for different bridge and navigation scenarios?

Approach

The review connects recent collision accidents with two protection pathways: active collision avoidance and passive anti-collision structures. Active systems are organised by input, analysis, and action, while passive structures are classified into six design families.

What the study contributes

  • A unified map from accident causes to active and passive protection measures.
  • A comparison of operating principles, advantages, limitations, and suitable application conditions.
  • Transferable concepts from adjacent fields, including vessel-vessel avoidance and metamaterial structures.

Physics-informed machine learning

Rapid ship-bridge collision safety assessment

Physics-informed multi-layer perceptron (MLP) model with data augmentation for ship-bridge collision safety assessment

Ocean Engineering 362, 126339 (2026) MLPData augmentationSHAP DOI

Research question

Can physical prior knowledge overcome a small real-world dataset and support rapid, interpretable screening of bridge anti-collision performance?

Approach

A dataset of 50 bridge assessment cases and nine ship, channel, and bridge features is expanded through two physics-informed strategies. MLP predictions are evaluated by repeated five-fold cross-validation, compared with SVM and random forest models, and interpreted using SHAP.

What the study shows

  • Both augmentation strategies improve the baseline model, with different precision-recall trade-offs.
  • The transformation-augmented MLP achieves 80% prediction accuracy.
  • Ship tonnage and navigable clearance width are the two most influential features.

Time-dependent reliability

Aged reinforced-concrete bridges under ship impact

Reliability assessment of aged reinforced concrete bridge under ship impact considering multi-hazards

Ocean Engineering 326, 120772 (2025) OpenSeesMonte CarloScour and corrosion DOI

Research question

How do scour, reinforcement corrosion, and uncertain vessel impacts change the collision reliability of a reinforced-concrete bridge throughout its service life?

Approach

An OpenSees finite-element model is coupled with vessel mass, velocity, and impact-angle distributions and Monte Carlo simulation. Time-dependent scour and material degradation are introduced to compare newly built and aged bridge states.

Time-dependent failure probability
9.86%at completion 30.20%after 20 years 50.95%after 100 years

Scour dominates the first two decades, while corrosion becomes increasingly important later; inspection around year 20 is therefore a key intervention point.

Crashworthy protective systems

Metamaterial fenders for monopile OWTs

Crashworthiness study of a monopile OWT with metamaterial-based fenders under vessel collisions

Engineering Structures 345, 121559 (2025) Nonlinear FEA22 MW OWTMetamaterial fender DOI

Research question

Can an annular metamaterial fender protect both a large monopile wind turbine and the striking vessel while maintaining acceptable nacelle acceleration?

Approach

Nonlinear finite-element simulations examine a 22 MW monopile OWT struck by a 5000 DWT vessel at 2-6 m/s. Baseline and dual aluminium-foam-ring fenders are assessed under multiple impact velocities, operational wind loads, and wind-impact angles.

What the study shows

  • The enhanced fender reduces OWT damage to negligible levels while keeping nacelle acceleration within the 6 m/s² safety threshold.
  • Vessel bow crushing depth is reduced by 14.53%.
  • Wind direction changes both peak acceleration and structural damage and must be included in collision-risk assessment.

Experimental, numerical, and analytical mechanics

Axial crushing force of bulbous bows

Study on the assessment of axial crushing force of bulbous bow for bridge against ship collision

Ocean Engineering 255, 111411 (2022) Axial crushing testsFinite elementsIUM DOI

Research question

Can bulbous-bow crushing force be estimated efficiently enough for bridge collision-risk analysis without constructing a detailed finite-element model for every passing vessel?

Approach

Two bow models with transverse plates and longitudinal stiffeners are tested under axial compression. Experiments and finite-element simulations validate an intersection unit method derived from rigid-plastic theory, with an equivalent-thickness correction for longitudinal stiffeners.

What the study shows

  • Internal structural arrangement and collapse mode strongly affect crushing force, even for bows with the same mass and external shape.
  • The finite-element model reproduces reasonable collapse modes and mean crushing forces.
  • IUM is suitable for transverse-plate bows, while longitudinally stiffened bows require the equivalent-thickness modification.
